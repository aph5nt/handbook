Where you meet it

      
Consul and Netflix Eureka are registries: copies register, the registry health-checks them, clients ask for the list. In Kubernetes, a Service gives one stable DNS (Domain Name System) name and virtual IP, and its endpoint list follows each pod's readiness check. A service mesh such as Istio or Linkerd moves the lookup into a sidecar proxy.

    

    

      
The practical choices

      
Prefer the platform's server-side discovery (a Kubernetes Service) unless you need smart client-side picking. Health-check every copy on a short interval. Deregister on graceful shutdown, before the process exits. Keep client caches short, and retry another copy when a connection is refused.

Interview line"Service discovery keeps a live list of healthy copies, either in a registry the client asks or behind one stable service address, and health checks plus deregistration on shutdown are what stop traffic going to a dead copy."
