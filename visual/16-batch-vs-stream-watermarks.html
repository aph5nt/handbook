The two numbers you choose

    
Watermark delay, 10 s here. How long the counter waits for stragglers before the first answer. Bigger means fewer late swaps, and a slower board.

    
Allowed lateness, 60 s here. How long a fired window keeps its count in memory, so a late swap can still correct it. Bigger means more corrections, and more memory.

    
After both. The swap goes to a side output. A nightly batch job recounts the day from storage, so the final numbers are exact.

  


  

    

      
Where you meet a stream

      
Flink, Kafka Streams or Spark Structured Streaming counting events per window. Flink names the same two knobs: forBoundedOutOfOrderness(10 s) and allowedLateness(60 s), plus sideOutputLateData. A Go chain indexer is a stream too: it reads up to the head minus a safety depth, so that depth is its watermark and it never sees late data.

    

    

      
Where you meet a batch

      
A nightly job that sums the day, moves old partitions to cheaper disks, or writes a quality report. A rerun replaces the whole day partition, so it is idempotent. It is also the safety net that catches everything the stream sent to the side output.
