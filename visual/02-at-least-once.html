At-least-once delivery and idempotency

A consumer reads a message, does its work, then acknowledges. If it crashes between the work and the acknowledgement, the message comes again. A wallet balance shows what that does to a careless consumer. Press Next.

Where you meet it: Kafka and SQS

      
Kafka consumers commit offsets after the work, and Amazon SQS redelivers after a visibility timeout. Both are at-least-once by default. The practical choice: make the effect idempotent, never trust "exactly once" alone.

    

    

      
Where you meet it: a graph sync

      
A graph sync merges each link by its key. Re-reading a block range after a restart changes nothing. A counter that adds instead of merging would double every link.
