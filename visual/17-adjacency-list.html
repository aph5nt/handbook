Where you meet adjacency lists

      
DozerDB and Neo4j keep each node's relationships with the node, so a hop costs O(degree). A StarRocks table of (from, to) rows is an edge list. A sort or index on from turns it into an adjacency list.

    

    

      
Where you meet a matrix

      
Small dense problems: all-pairs shortest paths (Floyd-Warshall) on a few hundred nodes, or the pixels of an image. Never a transfer graph: real graphs are sparse.

    

  


  

    
Side by side

MemoryO(V + E)O(V²)

      
Is there an edge a to b?O(degree of a)O(1)

      
List the neighbours of aO(degree of a)O(V): the whole row

      
FitsSparse graphs: almost all real onesSmall, dense graphs

      
TrapA supernode with millions of linksMemory explodes with V

      
Interview line"A matrix answers 'is there an edge?' in O(1) but costs V squared memory. A list costs V plus E and walks neighbours fast. Real graphs are sparse, so lists win."
