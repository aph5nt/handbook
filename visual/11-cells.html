Cells: a bad deploy breaks one slice, not everyone

A cloud service has a million customers. Instead of one big stack, it runs three identical cells, each with a fixed slice of customers behind a thin router. Watch a deploy go cell by cell, a poison request stay in one cell, and the one place cells cannot protect: the router. Press Next.

Where you meet it

      
AWS builds many of its own services from cells. Shopify calls them pods, Uber copied whole stacks into cells, and multi-tenant SaaS (software as a service) often puts each tenant in one cell. Shuffle sharding, used by Route 53, is a cousin.

    

    

      
The choice you make

      
Keep the router tiny: a lookup and a forward, deployed more carefully than anything else. Give each cell a tested maximum size. Run anything that needs every cell, such as a global report, outside the request path.
