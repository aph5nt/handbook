Aggregate root: one door, one rule, one commit

An Order holds its lines and its address. Only the Order itself may change them, because it guards one rule: the total stays under the customer's credit limit. Press Next and send it a command.

Where you meet it

      
A bank account with its entries: the account is the root and refuses an entry that overdraws. A Kubernetes Deployment with its ReplicaSets: you edit the Deployment, never a ReplicaSet by hand. A block in an indexer with its transactions and receipts: the block is written whole or not at all, and a "block indexed" event wakes the next stage.

    

    

      
The practical choice

      
Draw the aggregate around the rule that must be true at every instant. Keep it small: one root, the children it needs to check the rule, nothing else. Change one aggregate per transaction. Anything else learns about it through an event.

Interview line"An aggregate is the smallest cluster that must be consistent right now. Only the root takes commands, it enforces the invariant, and one transaction commits it. Everything beyond that border is eventually consistent through events."
