Distributed joins: broadcast, shuffle, colocate

StarRocks spreads billions of swaps over three backend nodes. Two rows can only be joined on the same node. Watch the three ways the engine brings matching rows together, and what a stale row count does to a broadcast.

Where you meet it

      
A 100-row DEX (decentralised exchange) registry against billions of swaps is a broadcast join. Swaps against transfers on tx_hash, both huge, is a shuffle, or no movement at all when both tables sit in one colocation group. Spark makes the same choice: broadcast hash join under a size limit, otherwise shuffle.

    

    

      
The practical choice

      
Keep statistics fresh, because the planner picks from row counts. Read the plan: EXPLAIN shows BROADCAST or SHUFFLE. For two big tables you always join on the same key, bucket both on that key with the same bucket count in one colocation group.
