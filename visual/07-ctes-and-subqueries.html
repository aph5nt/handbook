CTEs and subqueries: name a step, walk with a bound

An investigator asks where money went, up to three hops from one address. A recursive CTE (Common Table Expression) walks the transfers hop by hop. Watch the depth bound stop it, and watch what a cycle does without one. The second tab shows a subquery that runs once against one that runs once per row.

Where you meet it

      
Short, bounded walks in SQL: two or three hops for a report. Real graph walks over millions of addresses go to a graph database such as DozerDB (Neo4j-compatible), fed by a graph sync. Long reports read better as a chain of named CTEs, one step each.

    

    

      
The practical choice

      
Every recursive CTE gets a depth bound, WHERE h.depth < 3, or a visited set. Write correlated subqueries as a join against a grouped CTE when the planner may not do it for you, and check with EXPLAIN.
