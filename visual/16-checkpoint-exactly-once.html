Where you meet it: an indexer or a sync

      
A chain indexer keeps a checkpoint row per block range. A graph sync keeps a high-water mark per layer. Both write data first, then the mark, and key every row by the event.

    

    

      
Where you meet it: Kafka

      
A consumer commits its offset after its write, not before. Kafka transactions give true exactly-once only inside Kafka. Across systems, idempotent writes are the practical answer.
