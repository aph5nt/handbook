Where you meet balanced trees

      
Java TreeMap, C++ std::map and the Linux process scheduler use red-black trees. AVL trees suit read-heavy in-memory indexes. On disk, use a B-tree instead: one page holds hundreds of keys.

    

    

      
The choice you make

      
AVL is stricter: lookups a little faster, more rotations on insert. Red-black is looser: at most two rotations per insert, so writes are cheaper. Both keep every operation O(log n).
