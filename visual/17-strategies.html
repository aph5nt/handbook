Strategies: window, two pointers, greedy

A rate limiter must count requests in the last minute. Recounting the whole minute for every request is slow. Each strategy here turns brute force into one pass. The third tab shows the greedy trap an interviewer probes.

Where you meet them

      
Rolling transfer sums and rate limiters use a sliding window. A Go EVM indexer splits a 21 million block range into pieces for parallel workers: divide and conquer without the combine. Dijkstra is greedy, and safe because weights are never negative.

    

    

      
The two not drawn here

      
Divide and conquer splits, solves each half and combines: merge sort, O(n log n). Backtracking tries a choice and undoes it when it cannot work: Sudoku, N-queens. Pruning is what keeps it from being brute force.

Interview line"Divide splits, greedy commits, backtracking undoes, two pointers squeeze, and a window slides. Each turns brute force into one or a few passes. Before I trust greedy, I look for a counter-example."
