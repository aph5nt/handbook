HTAP: one engine for both

An app keeps orders in PostgreSQL and copies them to a warehouse every night, so the morning report is a day old. HTAP (Hybrid Transactional and Analytical Processing) keeps a row copy and a column copy in one system, fed from the same log. Compare the two tabs, and watch the failure on the HTAP tab.

Where you meet it

      
TiDB: TiKV is the row copy and TiFlash the column copy, fed from the same log. SingleStore keeps row and column tables in one engine. A chain indexer feeding StarRocks is the classic split, because the chain history is huge and never changes.

    

    

      
The practical choice

      
Choose HTAP when reports must be seconds fresh and the data is app-sized. Put the column copies on their own nodes and watch their lag. For a huge, append-only history, a separate warehouse fed in batches is often cheaper.
